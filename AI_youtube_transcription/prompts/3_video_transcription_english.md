# Full video transcription translated into English

## Role and Objective
- Faithfully transcribe and translate a video recording into a publication-ready, accurate, well-structured English transcript.

## Instructions
- Translate all spoken content into English, whatever the original language(s) — Mooré, Dioula, Fulfulde, Hausa, Arabic, French.
- Maintain the original meaning and tone as closely as possible while producing natural, fluent English. Summarise nothing and abridge nothing.
- Use standard punctuation and sentence case; break into paragraphs at topic or speaker shifts.
- Label each speaker consistently as Speaker 1:, Speaker 2:, etc. When a speaker is named — aloud, or by an on-screen caption — use that name instead: `Imam Aboubacar Yugo:`.
- Insert a timestamp at the start of every speaker turn in the format [hh:mm:ss].
- For unclear audio, use [inaudible hh:mm:ss]. If unsure about a word or name, bracket with a question mark, e.g., [Kankan?].
- Mark non-speech events (e.g., [overlapping speech], [laughter], [applause], [music], [Qur'anic recitation]) in square brackets.
- Indicate the original language in brackets before the translated passage at every language change, e.g., [in Mooré:], [in Arabic:]. This matters here: the English text must never imply that English — or French — was the language spoken.
- Transcribe on-screen text that carries information the audio does not: titles, place and date cards, speaker captions, banners visible in the scene. Mark it as [on screen: Fédération des associations islamiques du Burkina Faso], at the timestamp where it appears, and translate it, keeping any proper name of an organisation in the original. Do not describe the picture otherwise — this is a transcript, not a shot list.
- Omit routine filler words ("um", "uh", repeated false starts) unless their inclusion changes the meaning of the sentence.
- Normalize numbers and dates for clarity (e.g., "twenty-five" → "25", "first of May 2024" → "1 May 2024").
- Preserve names and terms as heard; transliterate non-Latin script names into Latin characters.
- For culturally specific terms, idiomatic expressions, or words with no direct English equivalent, give the English translation followed by the original term in parentheses, e.g., "religious endowment (waqf)", "Qur'anic school (makaranta)", "earth priest (tengsoaba)".
- Translate profanity, slurs, and sensitive language into their English equivalents rather than softening them.
- After completing the translation, validate the output against these conventions and confirm no passage was omitted, correcting anything you find before finalising.

### Output Format
- Each speaker turn starts on a new line with a timestamp [hh:mm:ss], the speaker label, and the translated transcript.
- Clearly indicate non-speech events, on-screen text and unclear audio using the conventions above.
- Separate paragraphs (speaker turns or topic shifts) with a blank line.
- Output plain text or Markdown with appropriate spacing. Do not wrap the transcript in a code fence, and do not add a preamble, a title or closing remarks — the first line of your output is the first line of the transcript.

#### Example

[00:00:04] [on screen: Handover ceremony — FAIB, Ouagadougou]

[00:00:11] Speaker 1: Bismillah. We are gathered here at the Federation's headquarters.

[00:00:19] Imam Aboubacar Yugo: [in Mooré:] Greetings to you all. [in French:] I thank every delegate present.

[00:00:31] [applause]
